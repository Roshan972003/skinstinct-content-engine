## Draft (REQUIRES YOUR REVIEW — not published, not sent anywhere)

A single INCI name can hide two completely different physical mechanisms.

If you read the ingredient list on a hydrating serum, you will almost certainly see "Sodium Hyaluronate" or "Hyaluronic Acid." What the label almost never tells you is the molecular weight distribution of that raw material.

Hyaluronic acid is a polymer, meaning it exists in chains of varying lengths, measured in Daltons (Da) or kilodaltons (kDa). Depending on the chain length, the molecule behaves differently on human skin:

The first thing to understand is high molecular weight hyaluronic acid (typically above 1,000 kDa). These are large molecules that cannot penetrate the stratum corneum. They remain on the surface of the skin, forming a breathable film that holds water and reduces transepidermal water loss (TEWL).

The second thing is low molecular weight hyaluronic acid (often below 50 kDa). These smaller chains can pass deeper into the upper epidermal layers to hydrate below the surface. However, if used in isolation without surface-active occlusives or higher molecular weight polymers, they do not provide long-term surface barrier support.

The third element is multi-molecular weight blending. Combining high, medium, and low molecular weight fractions allows a formula to address both surface water retention and deeper stratum corneum hydration simultaneously.

Yet under cosmetic labeling regulations, all three of these raw material variations appear as the exact same INCI name on the bottle. A serum formulated with 1% high molecular weight hyaluronic acid and a serum formulated with 1% low molecular weight hyaluronic acid share an identical ingredient list, but perform entirely differently on the skin.

I am not saying high molecular weight is inferior, nor am I saying low molecular weight is superior. I am saying that an ingredient name on a bottle is the beginning of the question, not the answer. Just as a label percentage on a niacinamide serum tells you very little without knowing the finished pH and delivery vehicle, "hyaluronic acid" tells you almost nothing without the weight distribution.

At Skinstinct, when we review raw material Certificates of Analysis (CoAs) with our contract manufacturer, we require documented specs on active concentrations, pH stability, and batch consistency before formulation begins. We publish our testing and sourcing detail on our website because customer transparency requires real data, not marketing shorthand.

If you are buying a hydrating serum, do not stop at reading the INCI list. Email the brand and ask for the molecular weight breakdown or Dalton range of the hyaluronic acid used. If they cannot provide that data from their raw material CoA, it tells you how much visibility they actually have into their own formula.
